[Lecture 12](../12-semester-project) gave the sequence of deliverables as a
checklist. This lesson runs one system through that exact sequence with the
artefacts actually written out, so you can see what each step produces and how
each one is built from the last.

The system is small enough to hold in your head and real enough to have the
tensions that make analysis worth doing.

## The situation

The **Riverside Food Bank** is a regional nonprofit. It takes in donated food —
from supermarket surplus, food drives, and a government commodities program — and
passes it to about 40 partner agencies: soup kitchens, shelters, and neighbourhood
pantries that hand it to households.

It grew from 12 partner agencies to 40 in three years. It still runs on a shared
stock spreadsheet, a paper clipboard for the weekly count, and an email inbox
where agencies send orders. In 2025 a manufacturer recalled a batch of peanut
butter and it took two staff three days of paper-tracing to work out which
agencies had received it. A funder has said that from 2027 it will require
lot-level traceability for every grant-funded item.

## 1. Frame the problem

**Goal.** Give the food bank one system that records every item from the moment
it is donated to the moment a partner agency receives it — so staff can see
current stock, allocate it fairly, trace any lot in a recall, and report to
funders without re-keying data.

**Objectives.** Specific and checkable; every later decision has to trace to one
of these.

- **O1** — Any staff member can see current stock by category and by lot in under
  two seconds, from a warehouse workstation or a phone.
- **O2** — Every outbound allocation is recorded against one partner agency and a
  specific set of lots, and no allocation can exceed recorded stock.
- **O3** — Given a recall notice, produce the list of affected lots and the
  agencies that received them in under ten minutes.
- **O4** — Generate the quarterly funder report directly from system data, with
  no manual re-keying.
- **O5** — A partner agency can place an order and see its status without phoning
  or emailing the warehouse.

**History.** The agency count more than tripled in three years while the tools
did not change. The 2025 recall exposed the lack of a link between a donated lot
and the agencies that got it. A funder has flagged that the quarterly report and
the annual audit disagree on totals, because the report is assembled by hand.

**Problems and opportunities, each paired with an objective.** If an objective
has no problem behind it, it is scope creep; if a problem has no objective, it is
being ignored.

| # | Problem or opportunity | Kind | Objective |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Stock is counted weekly on a clipboard; the spreadsheet is always stale | Problem | O1 |
| P2 | Allocations are agreed by email; two coordinators sometimes promise the same pallet | Problem | O2 |
| P3 | Nothing links a donated lot to the agencies that received it | Problem | O3 |
| P4 | The quarterly report is rebuilt by hand from three spreadsheets and the inbox | Problem | O4 |
| P5 | Agencies cannot see what is available, so they over-ask "to be safe" | Problem | O5 |
| P6 | Most donated cases already carry a GS1 barcode with a lot code; scanners are cheap | Opportunity | O1, O3 |

## 2. Describe the solution

**In prose.** A web application with two faces. In the **warehouse**, volunteers
scan cases in at the receiving desk; each scan creates an *inventory lot* tied to
a *donation*. Coordinators build *allocations* against live stock; the system
refuses an allocation that exceeds what is on hand and reserves the chosen lots
so no one else can promise them. Drivers get a pick list per stop and confirm
each delivery. In the **agency portal**, partner staff log in, see which
categories are available, place orders, and watch them move through a short set of
statuses. A reporting module rolls the movement history up into funder-ready
figures, and a recall search takes a lot code or barcode and walks forward to the
agencies that received it.

**Stakeholders.**

| Stakeholder | What they need from the system |
|---|---|
| Warehouse coordinator | Live stock; an allocation tool that cannot oversell; pick lists |
| Receiving volunteer | Fast scan-in, little typing, works on a tablet |
| Delivery driver | Today's route, a pick list per stop, one-tap delivery confirmation |
| Partner-agency staff | See what is available, order, track status, see their own history |
| Executive director | Quarterly and ad-hoc reports; a recall response; a fair-share view |
| Funder / auditor | Consistent, traceable pounds-in and pounds-out figures |
| Health authority | On a recall, proof of where a lot went and that it was pulled |
| Part-time IT volunteer | Something they can host, back up, and restore without a full-time admin |

**Rich picture — described.** The informal sketch would show the warehouse, the
donors feeding it, the agencies pulling from it, and the funder watching the
totals. It would also show the parts a clean diagram leaves out: two large
agencies have historically received more because their directors sit on the
board; the receiving volunteers are retirees who dislike fiddly screens; the
director wants "fair share" but has never put a number on it; and the 2027 funder
mandate sits over the whole thing as a deadline. The picture's job is to make
sure none of that gets designed away just because it does not fit a notation.

**Hardware and software.**

| Component | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| App hosting | Managed container platform, smallest tier | No full-time admin; predictable ~$30/mo |
| Database | Managed PostgreSQL | Transactions matter for "cannot oversell"; managed backups |
| Client devices | Two refurbished tablets (receiving, driver); staff use their own phones and PCs | Lowest cost; the tablet camera is the scanner |
| Barcode scanning | Camera-based for GS1 GTIN + lot, plus two USB scanners at the receiving desk | Cases already carry the barcodes |
| Accounts | Email and password for staff; one shared login per agency | Small user base; agency staff turn over often |

**Key technical features** — the headline functional requirements:

- Scan-in that builds a lot record from a GS1 barcode (product, lot, best-by).
- Allocation that is checked against live stock in one transaction and reserves
  the lots it uses.
- An agency ordering portal with status tracking.
- A forward trace from any lot or product code to the receiving agencies.
- A one-click quarterly funder report.

## 3. Plan and justify the project

**Methodology.** An **agile, incremental** build, justified against the table in
[Lecture 1](../01-introduction#choosing-an-approach): requirements will shift as
agencies react to the portal; there is a champion (the new operations manager);
the organisation needs something usable before the 2027 mandate; the director
accepts incremental delivery. But the requirements and the data model are
documented to full [SDLC](../01-introduction#the-development-life-cycle) standard,
because a funder audit expects a written specification and because the IT
volunteer is replaced every year or two and has to be able to pick the system up
cold.

**Work breakdown.** Built top-down as in
[Lecture 3](../03-project-management#breaking-the-work-down):

1. Discovery — interviews, shadow the receiving desk, collect the current forms
2. Analysis — requirements document, DFDs, data model, UML
3. Warehouse increment — scan-in, stock view, allocation, pick and confirm
4. Portal increment — catalogue, ordering, status, agency history
5. Reporting increment — funder report, recall trace, fair-share view
6. Rollout — migrate the spreadsheets, train volunteers, cut over
7. Project management and QA — throughout

**Activity list** (the spine of it), with durations, dependencies, and staff:

| ID | Activity | Days | After | Who |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Interviews and shadowing | 8 | — | Analyst |
| B | Requirements document | 5 | A | Analyst |
| C | DFDs, data model, UML | 7 | B | Analyst |
| D | Warehouse increment | 20 | C | 2 devs |
| E | Portal increment | 15 | D | 2 devs |
| F | Reporting and recall trace | 10 | D | 1 dev |
| G | Data-migration scripts | 6 | C | 1 dev |
| H | Training materials | 4 | E | Analyst |
| I | Pilot with five agencies | 10 | E, F, G | All |
| J | Full cutover | 3 | I, H | All |

**Schedule.** The Gantt chart lays these on calendar time; the network diagram
finds the [critical path](../03-project-management#scheduling): **A → B → C → D →
E → I → J**, totalling 68 working days, about 14 weeks. F and G run in parallel
and carry slack — but if E slips, the whole project slips, so E is where to watch.

**Feasibility.** Expenses and benefits, then a
[present-value table](../03-project-management#comparing-costs-and-benefits).

- *One-time:* analyst ~25 days at $400 ≈ $10,000; development ~85 person-days at
  $450 ≈ $38,000; two tablets and two scanners ≈ $700. **≈ $48,700.**
- *Recurring:* hosting, database, and a small support retainer ≈ **$3,000 / year.**
- *Quantified benefits / year:* coordinator time saved on the manual count and
  the allocation email, ~300 hours at $22 ≈ $6,600; the quarterly report from
  five days to half a day ≈ $3,200; less over-ordering ≈ $4,000; less spoilage
  from picking oldest-first ≈ $6,000. **≈ $19,800 / year**, so net of recurring
  cost, **$16,800 / year.**
- *Unpriced:* recall response from three days to under an hour, and meeting the
  2027 mandate — either of which alone justifies the project.

| Year | Cost | Benefit | Net | × PV (8%) | PV of net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 48,700 | 0 | −48,700 | 1.000 | −48,700 |
| 1 | 3,000 | 19,800 | 16,800 | 0.926 | 15,557 |
| 2 | 3,000 | 19,800 | 16,800 | 0.857 | 14,398 |
| 3 | 3,000 | 19,800 | 16,800 | 0.794 | 13,339 |
| 4 | 3,000 | 19,800 | 16,800 | 0.735 | 12,348 |
| 5 | 3,000 | 19,800 | 16,800 | 0.681 | 11,441 |
| | | | | **NPV** | **+18,383** |

Net present value over five years is about **+$18,000**; discounted payback is
about **3.4 years**. The return-on-investment chart would show cumulative net
crossing zero partway through year four. Given the mandate, the numbers only have
to show the project is not wasteful — and they do.

## 4. Discover the requirements

**Method, with justification.** Interviews plus **observation** — shadowing a
receiving shift and one delivery run — plus **document sampling** of the stock
spreadsheet, an email order thread, last year's funder report, and the 2025
recall paper trail. Not questionnaires: the population is tiny (eight staff, 40
agencies) and the work is physical, so watching it beats a form. One
[JAD](../04-interactive-methods#joint-application-design) session with the three
coordinators and the director settles what "fair share" means numerically.

**Functional requirements** — numbered so every later model can point back.

- **FR1** — Record a donation: donor, date, source type (retail, drive,
  government).
- **FR2** — On scan-in, create an inventory lot with product code, description,
  category, lot code, best-by date, unit count, and weight, linked to a donation.
  *(O1, O3)*
- **FR3** — Provide a manual entry path for items with no barcode. *(O1)*
- **FR4** — Show current stock by category and by lot, with total weight, updated
  on every movement. *(O1)*
- **FR5** — Create an allocation: choose an agency, add lines by category or by
  specific lot, with quantities. *(O2)*
- **FR6** — In one transaction, reject or reduce any allocation line that exceeds
  available (unreserved) stock. *(O2)*
- **FR7** — Reserve lots when an allocation is confirmed; release them if it is
  cancelled. *(O2)*
- **FR8** — Generate a pick list per allocation, ordered by warehouse location.
- **FR9** — Confirm a pick or delivery: move the stock out, stamped with the
  receiving agency, the timestamp, and the staff member or driver. *(O2, O3)*
- **FR10** — Agency portal: browse available categories with approximate
  quantities. *(O5)*
- **FR11** — Agency portal: submit an order and see its status — submitted,
  approved, being picked, out for delivery, received. *(O5)*
- **FR12** — Agency portal: view this agency's own order and receipt history.
  *(O5)*
- **FR13** — Recall search: given a product code or lot code, list every
  allocation and delivery that included it and the agencies involved. *(O3)*
- **FR14** — Quarterly report: weight received and distributed, by category, with
  a selectable date range and the agencies served. *(O4)*
- **FR15** — Fair-share view: distributed weight per agency per quarter against
  that agency's registered household count.

**Nonfunctional requirements** — each measurable, per the criteria in
[Lecture 4](../04-interactive-methods#what-a-requirement-is).

- **NFR1** — The stock view returns in under two seconds at the 95th percentile
  with a year of data (~60,000 lots).
- **NFR2** — Oversell rate is zero, enforced by a database constraint and proved
  by a concurrency test, not by a warning message.
- **NFR3** — A recall search returns complete results within ten minutes of staff
  effort (system time under five seconds).
- **NFR4** — The receiving flow is usable one-handed on a five-year-old tablet
  over warehouse Wi-Fi.
- **NFR5** — The database is backed up daily; a documented restore is tested
  quarterly; the recovery point is at most 24 hours.
- **NFR6** — An agency login can see only that agency's orders and history,
  verified by an authorisation test per role.
- **NFR7** — *Desirable, revisit after the pilot:* scan-in stays usable and
  queues during a 30-minute internet outage, syncing on reconnect. Flagged
  because its feasibility is unclear until the warehouse network is measured.

## 5. Model the processes

Built top-down as in [Lecture 8](../08-data-flow-diagrams).

**Context diagram.** One process — *Food Bank Distribution System*. External
entities: **Donor**, **Partner Agency**, **Delivery Driver**, **Funder**.
Warehouse staff are inside the boundary — they are the system's operators. Driver
is modelled as external because they touch only a narrow mobile view. Boundary
flows:

- Donor → donation manifest; System → Donor: donation receipt
- Partner Agency → order; System → Partner Agency: availability list, order status
- System → Driver: route and pick list; Driver → System: delivery confirmation
- System → Funder: quarterly report

**Level-0 diagram** — seven processes and six data stores:

1. **Receive donations** — in: manifest, scanned cases; writes `DONATION`,
   `INVENTORY LOT`, and an `IN` movement.
2. **Maintain stock view** — reads `INVENTORY LOT` and `MOVEMENT`; out: stock
   figures to staff.
3. **Handle agency orders** — in: order; writes `ORDER`; reads `INVENTORY LOT`;
   out: status to the agency.
4. **Allocate and pick** — in: an approved order; reads and reserves
   `INVENTORY LOT`; writes `ALLOCATION`; out: a pick list.
5. **Confirm delivery** — in: delivery confirmation; writes an `OUT` `MOVEMENT`;
   updates `ALLOCATION` and `ORDER`.
6. **Produce reports and recall traces** — reads `DONATION`, `MOVEMENT`,
   `INVENTORY LOT`, `AGENCY`; out: funder report, recall list.
7. **Manage agencies** — admin maintenance of `AGENCY`.

Data stores: `DONATION`, `INVENTORY LOT`, `MOVEMENT`, `ALLOCATION`, `ORDER`,
`AGENCY`.

**Level-1 child diagram for process 4, "Allocate and pick"**, decomposed and
[balanced](../08-data-flow-diagrams#child-diagrams-and-balancing) against its
parent:

- **4.1 Check availability** — reads `INVENTORY LOT`; rejects or trims lines that
  exceed unreserved stock.
- **4.2 Reserve lots** — writes the reserved quantity on `INVENTORY LOT`; creates
  the `ALLOCATION`.
- **4.3 Generate pick list** — reads `ALLOCATION` and each lot's location.
- **4.4 Record picked** — writes an `OUT` `MOVEMENT` with status "picked";
  updates `ALLOCATION`.

The inflow to process 4 (an approved order) and its outflows (the pick list, the
reserved-lot updates, the allocation record) all appear on the child. It
balances.

## 6. Model the objects

The [UML](../07-uml) set, each diagram feeding the next.

**Use-case diagram.** Context level: actors are Warehouse Staff, Partner Agency,
Driver, and Director. Subsystem level — five subsystems:

1. **Donation Intake** — log donation, scan case in, enter a non-barcoded item
2. **Inventory** — view stock, adjust or write off, search a lot
3. **Ordering** — submit order *(Agency)*, review order *(Staff)*, track order
   *(Agency)*
4. **Fulfilment** — allocate stock, reserve lots, generate pick list, confirm
   delivery
5. **Reporting & Compliance** — run funder report, run recall trace, view
   fair-share

**One use case, narrated — "Allocate stock".**

- **Actor:** warehouse coordinator. **Precondition:** an approved order exists.
- **Main flow:** (1) the coordinator opens the order; (2) the system shows each
  line with its available quantity; (3) the coordinator accepts the suggested
  lots or picks specific ones, oldest best-by first; (4) the coordinator
  confirms; (5) the system, in one transaction, re-checks availability, marks the
  chosen quantities reserved, creates an `ALLOCATION` linked to the order, and
  sets the order to "being picked"; (6) the system generates the pick list.
- **Alternate 3a:** a line exceeds available stock — the system highlights it and
  offers the lower quantity or a substitute category; the coordinator adjusts the
  line or splits the remainder to a back-order.
- **Alternate 5a:** another coordinator reserved one of the lots a moment earlier
  — the transaction's re-check fails, the system reloads availability, and the
  coordinator redoes the affected line.
- **Postcondition:** reserved stock never exceeds physical stock; the order is on
  the pick queue.

**Class diagram** — the classes the use cases reveal, with the attributes and
methods that matter:

- **Donation** `(id, date, donorName, sourceType)`
- **InventoryLot** `(id, gtin, description, category, lotCode, bestBy, unitCount,
  weightKg, reservedCount, location)` — methods `availableCount()`,
  `reserve(qty)`, `release(qty)`, `moveOut(qty)`
- **Agency** `(id, name, address, householdCount, active)`
- **Order** `(id, agencyId, submittedAt, status)`
- **OrderLine** `(id, category, requestedQty)`
- **Allocation** `(id, orderId, createdBy, createdAt)`
- **AllocationLine** `(id, lotId, qty)`
- **Movement** `(id, lotId, agencyId, type, qty, weightKg, timestamp, staffId)` —
  an append-only ledger; the reports and the recall trace read only this,
  `InventoryLot`, and `Agency`
- **User** `(id, name, role, agencyId?)`

Relationships: Donation `1—*` InventoryLot; Agency `1—*` Order; Order `1—*`
OrderLine; Order `1—0..1` Allocation; Allocation `1—*` AllocationLine;
AllocationLine `*—1` InventoryLot; Movement `*—1` InventoryLot; Movement `*—1`
Agency (null for `IN` and `ADJUST`).

**Sequence diagram — "Confirm delivery" (described).** Driver app →
System: `confirmDelivery(allocationId)`. System → Allocation: `markDelivered()`.
Loop over each AllocationLine: `InventoryLot.moveOut(qty)` and create a
`Movement(type = OUT)`. System → Order: `setStatus(RECEIVED)`. System → Driver
app: confirmation and the next stop.

**Activity diagram — order fulfilment (described)**, with
[swimlanes](../07-uml#activity-diagrams) Agency, Coordinator, System, Driver:
Agency submits an order → System validates it → Coordinator reviews *(approve /
adjust / reject)* → System reserves lots and produces the pick list → Driver
picks and delivers → Driver confirms → System closes the order and notifies the
Agency.

**Deployment diagram (described).** Browser and tablet nodes → HTTPS → one
application container on the managed platform → TCP → the managed PostgreSQL
instance. A nightly job copies the database backup to object storage. There is no
on-premises server.

## 7. Design what people touch

**Input design — the "Scan case in" screen** ([Lecture 11](../11-input-design)):

| Field | Control | Validation and behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Barcode | Auto-filled from the scanner; text field as fallback | Parse GS1 → product, lot, best-by; on a parse failure, expose the three fields for manual entry |
| Description | Text, pre-filled from the product catalogue when known | Required; remembered per product code |
| Category | Dropdown — Produce, Dairy, Protein, Grain, Canned, Non-food | Required |
| Best-by date | Date picker, pre-filled from the barcode | Required for perishable categories; warn if within seven days |
| Units in case | Number stepper, default from the catalogue | Integer ≥ 1 |
| Weight (kg) | Number, default = units × catalogue unit weight | Greater than 0; one decimal |
| Location | Dropdown of aisle codes, default `RECEIVING` | Required before the lot counts as shelved |

The screen is one-handed, has large targets, advances on the scan or the Enter
key, shows a running count of cases scanned this session, and needs no mouse.

**Output design — the quarterly funder report** ([Lecture 10](../10-output-design),
laid out to be scanned, with [honest presentation](../10-output-design#output-bias)):

- **Header** — food bank name, the quarter, the generated date, and the line
  "figures drawn directly from movement records".
- **Summary band** — total kg received, total kg distributed, agencies served,
  households reached.
- **Table 1, by category** — kg received, kg distributed, share of distribution.
- **Table 2, by agency** — kg received, households, kg per household, sorted by kg
  per household so an outlier is visible rather than buried.
- **Footnote** — any manual adjustments in the period and why.
- A plain bar for category share. No three-dimensional pie.

**CRUD matrix** ([Lecture 8](../08-data-flow-diagrams#crud-checking-the-model-against-the-data))
— processes against entities, checked for anything created but never read, or
read but never created:

| Process ╲ Entity | Donation | InventoryLot | Order | Allocation | Movement | Agency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Receive donations | C | C | | | C | R |
| Maintain stock view | | R | | | R | |
| Handle agency orders | | R | C U | | | R |
| Allocate and pick | | R U | R U | C | C | R |
| Confirm delivery | | U | U | U | C U | R |
| Reports and recall trace | R | R | R | R | R | R |
| Manage agencies | | | | | | C R U D |

Every entity is created somewhere and read somewhere. `Agency` is the only entity
with a delete, and only by an admin — deliberate. `Movement` is only ever updated
to flip "picked" to "delivered"; otherwise it is append-only, which is what makes
the audit trail trustworthy. The model passes.

## 8. Show it works

**Comparative results** — the system beside the realistic alternatives:

| Approach | Live stock | Cannot oversell | Lot traceability | Agency self-service | Funder report | Cost / year | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spreadsheets and email *(today)* | No | No | No | No | Manual, ~5 days | ~$0 plus labour | Breaks past ~20 agencies |
| Generic inventory SaaS | Yes | Partial | A lot field, no forward trace | No | Export and rebuild | $1–2k | No portal, no fair-share |
| Sector-specific hosted platform | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Built in | $6–15k plus onboarding | Strong fit; cost and data ownership are the trade-off |
| **This system** | Yes | Yes, transactional | Forward trace on an append-only ledger | Yes | One click | ~$3k | Fits the exact workflow and the 2027 mandate; needs a maintainer |

Where it does better, and why: transactional oversell prevention — a database
constraint, not a warning — and a forward lot trace built on an append-only
movement ledger, at roughly a third of the hosted platform's cost. The two things
the mandate and the audit both hinge on are the two things it is built around.

Where it does worse: there is no vendor support line. It depends on the IT
volunteer and on the written documentation to survive staff turnover — which is
exactly why steps 2 through 6 were documented to full SDLC standard even though
the build itself was agile.

<Note label="The thread to keep visible">
Take any class — say `AllocationLine`. It exists because the "Allocate stock" use
case (step 6) has to record which lot filled which order line. That use case
satisfies FR5 through FR7 (step 4). Those requirements serve objective O2 — no
allocation exceeds recorded stock (step 1). O2 exists because of problem P2, two
coordinators promising the same pallet (step 1). Walk it the other way and every
problem on the list ends in running code. Nothing on the class diagram is there
for a reason you cannot name.
</Note>

## Where this idea shows up now

The same case done today would produce a one-page brief instead of a history
section, a Linear project instead of a printed Gantt chart, a `schema` file
instead of a hand-drawn class diagram, and Figma frames instead of the
input-design table. The eight moves would be identical, and the traceability
thread would be the same thread. The [next lesson](../14-ai-and-system-design) is
about doing exactly this with an AI assistant in the loop — what it makes nearly
free, and what still has to be decided and checked by a person.
